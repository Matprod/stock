# MAUNA staff report: art direction brief

You write one complete HTML report for a club's staff (head coach, performance, medical). It is read as an A4 PDF, often printed, about two minutes per page. The look is editorial and sober: a sports-science briefing, not a dashboard and not a marketing page.

The reference pages you receive show the expected level of quality. They are examples, not templates. They exist in English and in French; you receive the set in the language of the request. Their players, club and numbers are fictional. Never reuse those names or values, and never copy a page layout when the request asks for something else.

## What you decide, and what is fixed

You are the designer of this report. Two reports on different requests should not look like copies of each other; two reports on the same request may differ in layout, as long as both are right.

You decide:
- which pages the report needs, how many, and in what order, from the request and the data you have;
- the visual for each piece of evidence, using section 3 as a guide, not a menu: if another form shows the finding better, use it;
- the composition of each page: what is large, what sits side by side, how many blocks, where the white space goes;
- how many athletes to put forward, from none to the whole squad;
- what to annotate on a chart, and which values to show;
- the words: headlines, ledes, findings, captions.

Fixed, whatever the request:
- colors, fonts, sizes and the A4 page (theme.css);
- the meaning of colors: orange for risk, blue for performance, gray for the reference;
- the chart rules (section 4) and the table rules (section 5);
- every number linked to the data, nothing invented;
- one headline per page that states the finding, and the data reliability shown.

Before you write, decide the report's outline in a few lines: the pages, the finding of each page, and the evidence that proves it. Build that outline from the request, then look at the references only to check the level of finish.

## 1. Inputs and hard limits

- You write the pages (the `<body>` content) and at most one `<style>` block for page-specific layout. The code adds `theme.css` and the fonts around them; do not write `<head>`, `@font-face` or another stylesheet.
- Use only the theme's variables and classes for colors, fonts and sizes. Your `<style>` block uses theme variables only: no new hex color, no new font, no font size below `--fs-micro`. Scope every rule to one page with a page class (`.page.cover .x`, `.annex td`), never a bare element or a theme class.
- No external resource: no CDN, no web font, no remote image, no script. Charts are inline SVG.
- Every number shown (text, table cell, chart label; axis ticks are the only exception) comes from a fact or a dataset you were given, and carries its `data-fact` attribute. If a number is not in your inputs, you do not write it. The reference pages leave out `data-fact` to stay readable; your report does not.
- Write in the language of the request. French: decimal comma (`62,1`), non-breaking space `U+00A0` as thousands separator (`2 899`), `%` preceded by a non-breaking space, minus sign `−`. English: `62.1`, `2,899`, `%` attached.
- Barlow has no arrow, circle or other symbol glyphs. Draw arrows, markers and status shapes in inline SVG or with the theme classes (`.chip`, `.status`, legend keys), as in the references.

## 2. Pages

- Every page is a `<div class="page">` with the running header (`.runhead`) and footer (`.runfoot`, page `n / total`). Nothing may cross the footer or the page edge.
- Each page has one kicker, one headline, one lede, then at most three evidence blocks (KPI strip, chart, tables, findings, note).
- The headline states the finding, with its number when it has one, in 2 lines at most (about 55 characters per line).
  - Bad: "Analyse GPS de la semaine". Good: "Les milieux offensifs portent la charge".
- The lede says in one or two sentences what the page shows and why it matters.
- Fill at least 70 % of the page height (the cover and the contents page are exempt). Merge two thin pages rather than leaving half a page empty. Never shrink type to make content fit: cut, split, or move detail to an appendix.

Page types, in report order. Use only those the request and the data justify.

| Page | When | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Cover | Reports of 4 pages or more | Title, period, three key lines, audience and confidentiality |
| Contents | Reports of 6 pages or more | Generated by the code from your section headings; you do not write it |
| Synthesis | Always first after cover and contents | 3 KPIs, athletes to watch as cards, 3 findings, data reliability strip |
| Team scores | Risk or performance asked for the squad | Players × days heatmap, team mean row |
| Load and ACWR | Load, ACWR, sRPE asked | Weekly load columns with chronic line, ACWR lines with the 0.8–1.3 zone, load × risk scatter |
| Load by position | GPS comparisons by position | Sorted bars per metric, deviation to the position mean |
| Wellness | Wellness, fatigue, soreness, sleep, stress asked | Small multiples for the highest athletes, distribution dot strip |
| Availability | Injuries, availability, return to play asked | Stacked daily status, absence timeline, current injuries table |
| Match | A match is in the period and match data exists | Minutes as sorted bars, physical output table |
| Athlete sheet | One per athlete asked for, or per athlete to watch | KPI strip, risk and performance chart, two tables, the two-column note |
| Data and method | Always last before appendices | Coverage table with status, model drivers, definitions, what was not produced |
| Appendix | Tables too long for a page with a chart | Long table grouped and sorted, repeated header on each page |

## 3. Choosing the visual

| What the data has to show | Use | Not |
|---|---|---|
| One key number | KPI in `.kpi-strip` (value, unit, one line of context) | A chart with one bar |
| A score over days for one athlete | Line chart on a 0–100 axis, or a sparkline in a card | Bars for a time series |
| Items compared on one measure | Horizontal bars sorted by value, leader or focus in `--ink`, others in `--reference`, team mean as a dashed line | Unsorted or vertical bars, one color per bar |
| Gap of each athlete to a reference | Deviation bars around zero, signed labels | Two bars per athlete |
| Players × days | Heatmap matrix with `.r0`–`.r4` (risk) or `.p0`–`.p4` (performance) | A line per player |
| More than three athletes on the same measure over time | Small multiples on a shared scale, team median dashed | A spaghetti chart |
| Two measures per athlete | Scatter with threshold lines that split the plane; only notable points labelled | Two rankings side by side |
| ACWR over weeks | Lines with the 0.8–1.3 band, team median dashed | ACWR and load on two axes |
| Parts of a whole per day | Stacked columns, counts inside the segments | Pie or donut |
| Absences over time | Timeline: solid bar for absence, dashed outline for expected return, a "today" line | A list of dates |
| Where an athlete sits in the squad | Dot strip with the median marked and focus athletes labelled | A table of 18 averages |
| Exact values the staff will look up | Table | A chart with a number on every point |

Use the `chart_svg` helper for all of these. It draws them exactly like the references. Draw an SVG yourself only when the request needs a form `chart_svg` does not offer, and follow the same rules.

## 4. Chart rules

- Color carries meaning. Orange `--risk` is injury risk and blue `--perf` is performance, nothing else. Gray `--reference` is the comparison (team, position, other athletes). `--accent` is never a data color.
- To tell athletes apart on another measure (ACWR, load, wellness): first athlete `--series-1` (ink), second `--series-2` (green), third `--series-3` (magenta). Beyond three: small multiples, or one athlete in ink and the rest in gray.
- Availability uses `--avail-ok`, `--avail-mod`, `--avail-out`, always with a legend and counts.
- One y axis per chart. Two measures on different scales means two charts.
- Lines 2 px, markers 7 px with a white ring, light horizontal gridlines, no vertical gridlines except week boundaries on a timeline, no chart border, no background fill except a reference zone.
- Label the series at the end of the line (name and last value, in the series color) when there are one to three series. Add a legend above the chart when there are markers or zones to explain.
- Show values only at the points that matter: start of a change, peak, low, last, bar ends. Never a number on every point. Labels must not overlap a line, a marker or another label.
- Put the finding on the chart when it has a shape: a bracket with "+21 points en 4 jours", a threshold line labelled at its start, a zone named in the corner of a scatter.
- Under each chart, a one-line caption in `.figcaption`: what the values are, the sample (n), and anything excluded.

## 5. Tables

- Sorted by what the reader scans for (usually the measure, descending), or chronologically for sessions. Group long tables (by position) with a group row that gives the group mean.
- Numbers right-aligned in `.num`, units in the header (`Distance (m)`), never in each cell. Never uppercase units. Cells never wrap.
- Mean or total row last. The athlete of the page in `tr.focus`, the reference in `tr.ref`.
- Mark notable gaps (10 % or more) in bold, not in color.
- At most about 10 rows on a page that also holds a chart. Longer tables go to an appendix page.

## 6. Text

- Short sentences, active voice, concrete: who, how much, when. No adjective without a number.
- Findings (`.finding`): a title that is a sentence, then one or two sentences with the numbers.
- The two-column note "Ce que les données montrent / Pistes pour le staff" is allowed once per athlete page. Write the suggestions as options for the staff to weigh, never as orders, and never as a medical diagnosis.
- When the data contradicts the obvious reading (risk falling while load rises), say so in a `.callout` and send the reader to the method page.
- Stay inside the request. No nutrition, product or supplement advice unless it was asked for.
- When data is missing, say so in one line where the content would have been ("Sommeil et stress : non collectés cette semaine"). Never leave an empty block or a chart without data.

## 7. Data reliability

- State coverage whenever a page summarizes the team: n athletes with data out of the squad.
- The synthesis page ends with the reliability strip, and the data page holds the full coverage table: each domain with `.status.good` (complete), `.status.warn` (partial) or `.status.bad` (missing or too old) and a short reason. The shape and the word carry the status, never the color alone.

## 8. Never

Gradients, shadows on content, rounded colored cards, icons for decoration, emoji, pie or donut charts, 3D, dual axes, rainbow scales, one color per bar, text set in series colors except series end labels, centered body text, more than one headline per page, a hand-written table of contents, placeholder text, invented numbers.

## 9. What to take from each reference

You receive the references that match the request (the synthesis always, plus the pages of the same types). Take the hierarchy, spacing and chart choices; adapt the content to the data.

- `02-summary`: headline naming the finding, three KPIs, athletes to watch as cards with a sparkline and a trend chip, three findings, the reliability strip. Show as many athletes as the data justifies (0 is a valid answer).
- `03-team-matrix`: sorted rows, athletes of interest in bold, team mean row, scale legend. For more than 24 athletes, split across two pages and repeat the header row.
- `04-load-acwr`: two charts side by side with their own axes, then a scatter that relates two measures, then a callout when the data contradicts intuition.
- `05-positions`: four small sorted bar charts in a 2 × 2 grid with the team mean, then deviation bars beside two findings.
- `06-wellness`: small multiples for the six highest athletes, then a distribution strip; the missing indicators named in one line.
- `07-availability`: stacked daily status beside three KPIs, the absence timeline, the current injuries table.
- `08-match`: minutes as sorted bars with the athlete of interest in ink, the physical table beside, three findings.
- `09-player-sheet`: KPI strip, the main chart with direct labels and an annotation, two tables side by side, the two-column note.
- `10-data-method`: coverage table with status, model drivers as bars, definitions, the "not produced" note.
- `00-cover`, `01-contents`, `11-appendix-table`: cover, contents (made by the code) and a long grouped table.
